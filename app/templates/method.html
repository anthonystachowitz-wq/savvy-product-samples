Method

How these samples are built

The point of the samples is not the pages. It is the way they were built: real model calls, recorded so they replay, small evaluations, and an honest list of what failed.

The model and the provider

All model work runs on DeepSeek through its API. The default model is DeepSeek Flash, with DeepSeek V4 Pro available for the harder generation steps. Every output shown in the samples was produced by a real call, saved to a recording with its model, latency, and token usage, and replayed by default so a reviewer never sees a spinner fail.

Where the deployment allows it, a module can make a fresh live call inside a small daily budget. The budget exists so a public demo link cannot run up an API bill.

Model outputs are generated text, not advice, and every client-facing output sits behind a human approval step and an evidence record.

How this was built in two days

Two days, five modules, 179 tests. Transition Copilot was built the day before the other four. Every module shares the same platform: the DeepSeek client and recorded-run cache, the audit log, the module state, and the suite layout.

I made the product decisions, wrote or directed every prompt, defined the guardrails and the evaluations, reviewed the output, and ran the QA. AI coding agents wrote most of the code. That division of labour is the workflow I would bring to the role: the leverage is real, and so is the need to review, test, and keep a human accountable for what ships.

This is a work sample, not a proposal for Savvy's roadmap. You may already have some of this internally. If so, the useful part is the method: what I noticed, what I chose, how fast it came together, and how the controls are built.

The guardrails that repeat everywhere

  • Every output that reaches a client or a custodian is held for a named human approval.
  • Every number in a draft points at a seeded source field, or the draft is flagged.
  • Client-facing work for a household without protocol consent is blocked on the server.
  • Advice language is blocked. Anything that looks like a recommendation routes to the adviser.
  • Each approval writes an evidence record and an audit event.

What this shows, and what it does not

  • Shows: speed with AI, product judgment across five surfaces, control design, and honesty about model failures.
  • Does not show: production engineering depth, a held-out benchmark, or any custodian, CRM, or private markets integration.
  • The interview question I expect: what did you do versus the agents. Answer: every product decision, prompt, guardrail, evaluation, and review. The agents typed most of it, and I am accountable for all of it.

What this is not

  • Not a Savvy product, and not affiliated with Savvy.
  • Not connected to a custodian, a CRM, or a private markets administrator.
  • Not a measurement. The hours and margin figures are modelled estimates.
  • Not legal or compliance advice. The policy references are synthetic.
