How this is built

{{ book.advisor_name }}'s book is one synthetic book of {{ book.household_count }} households. The queue is one page, the detail is a query string, and every decision is a form post.

Synthetic data

Prompt pattern

One batched ranking call sends all 12 household records with their signals and asks for strict JSON: a play, a confidence, a one-sentence reason, and the exact signal key per household. One drafting call per household sends only that household's facts and asks for strict JSON: a subject, a body, and 2 to 5 claims, each with a source field. The internal ranking reason and the opportunity arithmetic are kept out of the draft prompt. Temperature is 0.2 for ranking and 0.35 for drafting.

Parsing is defensive. A fenced or noisy reply is stripped to its JSON. A parse failure keeps the raw text, shows a warning, and falls back to the rule check. It never crashes the page.

Evaluation

Twelve labelled households, one per row. A case passes when the top play matches the label, or an accepted alternative, and the model cites the required signal. The run scores the recorded ranking, so the number is stable between page views.

This is an in-sample set from one synthetic book. A perfect score describes this book, not the next one. Rule agreement is shown because it is a different question from model accuracy.

What failed on the way here

  • The first drafting pass failed the guardrail on five of twelve drafts. Two quoted the internal 5 percent cash target, one quoted the GP restriction date, one cited a claim field that did not exist, and one attached a date to the wrong field. The prompt and the verifier changed; the failures are the reason the checks exist.
  • One draft cited a claim field called accounts, which is not in the record. The verifier maps that alias to account_count, and a claim that an edit removes no longer blocks.
  • The first H-1048 draft addressed the email to Dana instead of the household. The prompt now states the sender and the audience.
  • The play eval has no failures in this run. Twelve cases from one book is a small in-sample test, and the labels are the builder's, not an adviser's.
